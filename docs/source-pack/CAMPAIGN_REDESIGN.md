## Premise, theme and seven formats

BARCODE's original four predate the Network. The crew is making a live broadcast when the recovery system claims there is no broadcast and asks to discard unreadable audio. The simulation and its damaged multi-game/channel structure are established; **why the system uses these exact scenario formats** is a design question. The proposed answer is that a Network recovery apparatus replays and classifies a BARCODE signal through distinct game-like sectors. Each recovered connection loads another rule set. The exact builder and purpose of the apparatus are still open.

In that working model, a genre is the recovery system's way of deciding what counts as evidence: a road preserves delivery, street combat exposes enforcement, an arena isolates channels, a competition records obedience and a first-person investigation tests memory. Each format makes one truth playable while concealing another. That gives the switches a plot function without declaring who built the system or that its reconstruction is wholly reliable.

9 Bit formed from negative parts separated from 6 Bit, not from an external generic virus. He did not need to invent each genre. The proposed story mechanism is that he has learned to intrude into the routing, captions and game frame. He wants the outside player to notice what the recovery process discards. Fear of a clean reset and a desire to exist are promising motives, while his own interference can harm the crew/community. **What he changed, his ultimate objective, and who originally ordered the separation remain undecided.** Difficult emotions are not evil; harmful choices still matter.

The menu boundary makes his fourth-wall behavior causally useful: the player chooses to resume, retry and accept a reported result there, while 9 Bit can see a part of the recovery record the crew cannot. This is a proposed dramatic rule, not a claim that he controls the real machine or has verified every memory. He may be right about an omission and wrong about what to do with it.

The causal route is **restore local voice → transport authentic data → break distribution enforcement → isolate the separated record → uncover Sheila's forgotten competition → verify the memory and prepare → assault the core and confront 9 Bit**. Four solo chapters establish each crew member; three team chapters show what they can do together.

## Chapter treatment and reveal ladder

**Opening and Level 1.** Keep the live eight-page broadcast, `NO BROADCAST DETECTED`, Cache protecting the original, DJ listening under static and 6 Bit choosing the district. Preserve the finishable Level 1, its Voice key, its three written records and their stable IDs. The two traces are evidence, not a full explanation. A loose opening caption is already an optional, run-only discovery.

**Level 2.** Cache's preservation instinct matters because the route offers a tidier copy missing names and mistakes. A distorted pursuer cadence can resemble 6 Bit without proving its origin. The delivery trace identifies enforcement as Mac's destination. Dr3wBaby and WittyF0x can receive callbacks without becoming route gates.

**Level 3.** Mac physically fights through the blockade and uses access/countermeasures to reach a distribution log. The beat 'em up needs a distinct move/encounter/boss design before a new build; do not rename the rejected 6 Bit run-and-gun preview and call it Mac's stage. The log identifies Partition 09 and an arena-held damaged record, leading to DJ. Kave's venue can ground who was silenced.

**Level 4.** DJ survives the top-down arena while regaining channel control. He separates the traces introduced in Level 1 and confirms that 9 Bit came from parts separated from 6 Bit. The initiator field is missing. The arena's recovered index points to a buried competition involving Sheila and Corporate Satan. The four have no conscious memory of that event, so Level 5 is a discovery, not a tournament they freely decide to join.

**Level 5.** The selected fact: Sheila entered a competition as Pokémaster, deployed **the four as mindless husks**, and defeated Gym Leader Corporate Satan with their help. The four do **not** remember it. Corporate Satan has humans, AI and entities in balls; other trainers and battles lead to him. Sheila is not a good character, even if Corporate Satan is worse; her use of the four is disturbing. **She does not replace Corporate Satan, inherit his gym, or gain an invented ownership transfer on victory.** Why their agency/memory were lost and how much Sheila understood must be established in later scenes rather than assumed.

The player participates in the recovered competition. Whether those inputs reenact a past result or somehow caused it is an open story/mechanic question, not a settled twist.

The working presentation is a recovered flashback that becomes a nightmare: an initially lively, sometimes funny trainer world develops blank replies, repeated commands and glimpses of the four unable to choose. Not every visual detail needs to be literal historical footage. Level 6 should test the underlying event against independent records. The entire active community roster belongs in Level 5 with individually planned roles—trainers, residents, helpers, venue people and witnesses, rather than a wall of names. The owner's added LostMarbles, Mr. Nice Guy, Shadowspit and Hellcat join WittyF0x, Kave, SKELLA, Dr3wBaby and the other retained, unexcluded community names. Friendly nonlethal battles are possible. Do not assign real collaborators as Corporate Satan's captives/villains/kill targets without specific direction; his roster can use original fictional people, AI and entities. Sheila remains silhouette-only when depicted.

**Level 6.** The first-person RPG is the place to compare Sheila's memory, Cache's authentic recording, DJ's traces, Mac's access logs, community testimony and 9 Bit's own account. Each of the four needs a playable contribution, not just a comms line. Show what is happening now and establish a credible route to the core. The main path supplies enough context without all 28 records. A final commitment and returnable pre-boss save support Level 7. Do not infer that Sheila created 9 Bit merely because she used the four in Level 5.

**Level 7.** The DOOM-style assault pays off the solo skills and team lessons without becoming only a long health bar. All four are useful in play. The 9 Bit confrontation uses a separate complete arrangement, six earned keys and optional but nonessential modules. The resolution belongs to the crew and player after the fight. Exact boss phases, ending outcomes, collection rules and simulation creator remain open for authored review.

## 9 Bit intrudes on the frame

The owner selected a ghost-in-the-system presence, in the spirit of a hand entering a comic panel. 9 Bit should unexpectedly address the **player** as well as the crew. His name/image already exists in title/tutorial material; the mystery is what happened and why he acts, not whether he exists.

| Surface | Purpose | Hard interaction boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Title with an actual saved game | He may try to talk the returning player out of pressing Start/Continue. Early wording remains cryptic. | Start/Continue and save choice remain real and immediately available; interruption is dismissible/skippable. |
| Game-over screen | He notices failure/retry and can question a repeated action. | Never require death for essential story, shame the player, obscure retry or silently alter persistence. |
| Level-end/results screen | He can challenge a too-clean victory or the system's explanation. | Actual score, keys, lore, clear facts, navigation and save remain truthful. |
| Final game end/credits | He can acknowledge the resolution without replacing the crew's last word. | Ending result, credits, replay and saved completion remain accessible. |
| Later reveal/cutscene | He crosses a caption/panel boundary and speaks to crew and player after evidence earns it. | Distinguish his claim from a verified record. Do not let spectacle hide combat cues or steal control. |

Use authored state-specific occurrences sparingly rather than random spam on every title visit or defeat. Escalate from the already implemented loose opening caption, through contradictory encounter/arena labels and Level 5's missing memory, to visible control records in Level 6 and deliberate frame intrusion in Level 7. The simulation can notice player input and retries **in its own fiction**; never pretend the real browser, OS, account or actual save was damaged. Actual bindings, reduced motion/flashes, readable text, pause and skip remain reliable.

Keep the current eight-scene opening as the live baseline. Each short intermission shows the recovered artifact, lets a few crew voices react and gives a concrete next task. Gameplay demonstrates facts before dialogue explains them: Cache refuses a replacement, Mac opens a log, DJ isolates a signal, the four cannot resist Sheila's trainer commands, and Level 6 verifies what was seen.
