Face inputs are synchronization pieces, not combat commands. Version 4 reuses
the version-3 lane chart, immutable announced pad addresses, heard timing,
captures, extensions, stacked arrangement and existing passive synchronization
bonuses. Four live parts mean full synchronization: peak driving momentum,
more offensive power and the smallest enemy tracking footprint. There is no
replacement meter or retuned music timeline. The five aligned source tracks
and the full 100-bar recording remain the musical and delivery clock.

The four skills recharge independently and work at zero synchronization.
Turbo retains the next-ONE physical launch so a press cannot relocate an
announced pad. Attack targets actual nearby foes or sends a visible forward
shot; Defend is a timed contact counter; Disrupt interrupts enemy tracking and
committed pressure. Direction, proximity and timing matter, independently of
the musical catch. Poor synchronization reduces advantages, not access to
fighting. Existing passive traffic contact buffers remain synchronization
benefits; new combat foes use explicit combat defense.

## Chase and completion

Fresh chapters carry encounterVersion 4 and a separate combat state. Bikes
and rammers introduce conflict, escorts and disruptors pressure later routes,
then a persistent rig exposes three sequential systems with real health.
Committed warnings precede contact; dodging produces a recovery opening but
does not damage the rig by itself. Attacks, defended contacts and actual Turbo
contacts earn damage and takedowns. Wrecks, flips, rider ejection and bounded
chain reactions belong to the same physical road and rearview.

The delivered-original ending requires actual rig defeat and the full song.
Existing measured beat accuracy, connected catches, best combo, clean bars and
stacked score determine the level's earned points; combat awards use the live
stack multiplier. The ending receipt freezes the measured attempt. Save
validation binds damage, sequential systems and defeat to genuine combat
history and rejects unsupported future versions. Retry retains checkpoint
progress while discarded partial attacks cannot replay damage.
