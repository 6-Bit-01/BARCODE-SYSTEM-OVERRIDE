## What carries the player through seven genres

The crew sets out to restore a silenced broadcast. Repairing its six damaged sectors exposes evidence of a separation that the recovery story cannot explain. The objective becomes restoring enough access and context to confront 9 Bit, including the harm he now chooses to cause. Winning means regaining agency over the signal and deciding its future, with the full crew involved.

The simulation's damaged cartridge/channel architecture explains the changing rules. Each transition follows a recovered connection or a necessary next operation: **restore a local signal → transport its surviving data → break a distribution blockade → reconstruct the damaged record → meet the people/programs that record affects → reach the control infrastructure → confront 9 Bit with the recovered context**. Do not replace those links with seven unrelated genre jokes.

| Story span | Player expectation | Change in understanding |
|---|---|---|
| Opening and Levels 1–2 | Repair the broadcast and keep the crew connected | The Network's labels and its surviving records disagree |
| Levels 3–4 | Find the source of the contradiction | An intentional separation occurred; the record still does not establish who ordered it |
| Levels 5–6 | Hear the other side and stop the active harm | 9 Bit has a relationship to 6 Bit, but his claims are not automatically the whole truth |
| Level 7 | Prepare, confront and choose with context | Crew history, recovered tools and the player's discoveries have concrete payoffs |

9 Bit is already depicted/named in existing title/tutorial material. The mystery is what happened and what he wants, not a false promise that the player has never heard his name. The future intro/title/tutorial pass must align these disclosures deliberately. Do not quietly remove the title art to manufacture a reveal.

## Level-by-level treatment

### 1 — Dead Air District / 6 Bit / platforming and rhythm combat

**Goal:** restore a neighborhood’s ability to transmit. The current tutorial, four encounter groups, roofs, lift, twenty mission defeats, sixteen-hit Jammer and boss route remain the playable spine.

**Progression:** begin with a local failure the player can visibly improve; teach the existing threats; make the upper route and preserved memories worth noticing; destroy the Jammer; let the restored district make the success felt; resolve the existing boss encounter. A restored outgoing connection supplies the story reason for transport in Level 2. First key: Voice. Optional module: Beat Anchor; its exact challenge remains a future design and it is not silently the current Signal Amp.

**Crew/cameos:** the three existing records give Cache Back, DJ Floppydisc and Mac Modem real voices. Working additions: Cliff's questionable maintenance note; Sheila's practical intercom correction; one Studio Rat sighting and a WittyF0x route hint. Do not crowd the tutorial with introductions.

**Story turn:** two opposing waveforms can produce apparent silence without either disappearing. This is evidence, not proof of the simulation creator or a full explanation of 9 Bit.

**Set piece:** the established restoration wave and boss entrance are the centerpiece. Comic captions/frames can react to this real event; they must not obscure the counter, steal control or reset the clock. Its restored signal later returns as a Full Mix layer.

### 2 — The Cache Line / Cache Back / Rad Racer direction

**Goal:** carry surviving signal/data through a failing transit route to the distribution sector. Cache Back's preservation instinct matters when the route tries to substitute a clean-looking but incomplete copy.

**Progression:** learn immediate steering and traffic; choose an optional service route; survive one phrase-driven pursuit/road event; deliver the surviving copy. A distorted pursuer transmission resembles 6 Bit's cadence without yet proving its origin. Key: Bass. Optional Phase Capacitor supplies a later transit shortcut/evasive option.

**Crew/cameos:** Dr3wBaby's proposed service-bay/decal/transmission presence establishes a recognizable collaborator without making him the transport system's owner. WittyF0x can provide the optional-route callback if already met. The required route always exists independently of a cameo or secret.

**Story turn and exit:** preservation includes inconvenient detail. The delivered data exposes a distribution blockade, giving Level 3 a destination and a reason to fight. Cache's later finale role protects the original record while the crew reconstructs it.

**Set piece/music:** road geometry and traffic respond to sections of Level 2's own track; input is never withheld until a beat. A torn road-frame transition is presentation over proven geometry, not a required new movement power.

### 3 — Broadcast Slum / 6 Bit / Contra direction

**Goal:** break the distribution blockade so the recovered material can reach a reconstruction system. This is the first new genre to prototype, even though it is third in the working story order.

**Progression:** immediate movement/fire, a small readable weapon vocabulary, one escalating broadcast-defense set piece and a final transmitter encounter. Key: Drums. Optional Breach Emitter later opens the armory/weakens an armor phase.

**Crew/cameos:** Kave's proposed venue or hidden relay provides a short support scene and a later archive-room callback. Other community posters are background candidates; no guest is converted into an enemy or capturable creature.

**Story turn and exit:** a surviving record identifies a rejected response profile/Partition 09. The crew obtains a damaged separation record, but it cannot yet be read reliably. Its reconstruction is the concrete purpose of Level 4.

**Set piece/music:** volleys and stage events can use phrase cues while fire and movement remain responsive. An impact can knock a broadcast caption into another panel during a safe, authored transition; the actual attack rules remain legible.

### 4 — Buffer Underflow / DJ Floppydisc / Tetris or Dr. Mario direction

**Goal:** reconstruct the damaged record without treating every unusual fragment as corruption to delete. Linked-pair patching remains the recommended branch, not an invented final selection.

**Progression:** teach symbol/shape rules; introduce interference; reconstruct a useful signal through cascades; complete the reconstruction challenge. Key: Synth. Optional Restore Point later grants a bounded recovery/rollback.

**Crew/cameos:** SKELLA's proposed visual sample-bank/chorus association fits this stage; a new audible music/voice contribution requires an actual supplied/cleared asset, not an assumed permission or a placeholder claim. Studio Rats can rearrange an optional practice-board detail without changing rules mid-judgment.

**Story turn and exit:** the separation record is real; its missing initiator field stays unresolved. The waveform metaphor from L1 pays off mechanically: isolation can reveal what a combined signal concealed. A remaining source points to the preserve and those affected by the process.

**Set piece/music:** clears restore this level's arrangement. The player solves a puzzle rather than performing the same timed attack used in Level 1. A misplaced caption can snap into a harmless practice slot as an optional Comix Zone callback.

### 5 — Signal Preserve / original four / Pokémon direction

**Goal:** investigate surviving Samples/programs and compare the partial record with lived evidence. Collect/equip a compact set of musical/program Samples on the four crew members; do not add community members to a capture roster.

**Progression:** one small settlement, one route, a few encounters, a compact party loop, then the preserve's central confrontation. Initial content ceiling remains 6–8 Sample families. Key: Samples. Optional Crew Link adds a coordinated reaction and deeper crew context later.

**Crew/cameos:** all four must contribute differently. Kave's venue, Dr3wBaby's earlier mark and SKELLA's motif can return as concise callbacks. WittyF0x can point out a discrepancy. These scenes are working placements; reuse each introduction instead of giving everyone a long new origin speech.

**Story turn and exit:** proposed first substantive direct contact from 9 Bit. He describes himself as what was rejected; the crew can hear the claim without endorsing every accusation. Evidence and present harm point toward the control infrastructure. The missing initiator is not filled by an arbitrary exposition terminal.

**Set piece/music:** Sample loadouts and turn choices change the arrangement. A familiar captured-program or naming joke can react to player choices without using a guest likeness, copied creature or mandatory joke detour.

### 6 — Firewall Cathedral / Mac Modem with crew assists / DOOM direction

**Goal:** breach the control infrastructure and expose the route to the actual confrontation. Mac's access/risk identity earns the lead; he is not silently rewritten as the origin of the catastrophe.

**Progression:** establish first-person movement, navigation, readable threats, doors/switches and a small weapon/ability set; reach the control encounter. Key: Noise/FX. Optional Root Credential supplies map/control access and removal of one finale hazard.

**Crew/cameos:** Cliff's earlier maintenance detail can become a practical route payoff; a Kave secret-room marker is an optional callback. Neither is required for progress. Sheila/BNL can communicate bounded known status; they do not know every secret because they have a radio.

**Story turn and exit:** proposed full 9 Bit appearance and confirmed location. His current actions put others at risk. The six guaranteed keys restore access to the finale; the crew's recovered connection presents the route. **No W3T TDDY appearance or seventh credential requirement.**

**Set piece/music:** first-person action remains responsive; the independent track provides threat/section cues. A simulated maintenance corridor can briefly expose the broadcast frame as architecture after the navigation proof works. Do not manufacture system-error screens that block real controls.

### 7 — Negative Space / original four / first-person RPG finale

**Goal:** prepare, reconstruct the mandatory context, find 9 Bit, fight, and resolve the signal's future. Reuse the proven first-person technology; settle combat cadence with a playable command/combat proof.

**Progression:** central mixing/preparation hub → required reconstruction/main descent → explicit final commitment → 9 Bit encounter → authored resolution and credits. Optional archive, transit, armory, rollback, memory and admin wings pay off the six modules. The final pre-boss save remains reachable; earlier content is revisitable before commitment.

**Crew/cameos:** each original member owns an essential contribution. The crew preserves evidence, separates signals, opens access and makes the decision together. Sheila can support operations from a silhouette/intercom. Studio Rats and the named collaborators pay off established moments; they do not displace the four from the climax.

**Confrontation proposal:** reflection/decoys → compact callbacks to the earlier genres → a close confrontation with the complete arrangement. The fight is beatable with no optional modules. Musical callbacks use Level 7's own verified arrangement, never simultaneous playback of six unrelated songs.

**Resolution boundary:** the creator/initiator, final choice structure, actual ending outcomes and hidden collection evaluation remain open. Use a development stub in the future prototype; do not ship candidate endings as approved canon. Difficult emotions are not themselves the villain; harm still has consequences. Required context must be intelligible without collecting all 28 records.
