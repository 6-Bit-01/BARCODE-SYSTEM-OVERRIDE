# Bounded audio resume — October 6

Source inspection confirmed that `AudioSystem.resumeRuntimeAudio()` directly awaited the browser's `AudioContext.resume()` with no deadline. A pending device promise therefore left both the shared lifecycle transition and `PauseMenu.resumePending` occupied indefinitely. The pause RAF continued accepting menu input, but further Resume requests joined that pending operation. This matches the reported native browser pause screen; the repaired browser build still needs a fresh native check.

Resume now has a one-second deadline in the existing audio-owned timeout registry. Mac's combat simulation does not depend on a beat clock, so it resumes play with an explicit `audioDegraded` receipt if sound remains unavailable. A delayed device response may restore the existing transport/beat sync only while that same audio generation and lifecycle session are still running. Pause, stop, title return, and a fresh generation cancel the request. A late hardware activation after a newer pause is suspended without awaiting it or starting a gameplay owner. Diagnostics expose pending/deferred state and the last audio resume receipt.

The existing keyboard/click sound-recovery listener also uses this guarded owner during running Mac play. A new gesture may retry the delayed device; an old promise cannot restart beats a second time or undo a newer pause. The legacy context-only listener checks generation/context and newer pause after its await without starting music.

Cache road has no silent drive clock: its `updateDrive` requires a running transport while its race timer continues independently. Level 1 rhythm rejects unavailable judgments. Those modes therefore receive a bounded, retryable failure and retain their frozen paused beat clock; the menu remains usable. This change does not invent a substitute song clock.

The shared loop's existing `resumeGame()` now runs before lifecycle compatibility projection clears `isPaused`, so it can reset frame time and reschedule its sole RAF. Title return may cancel an in-flight resume. Mac pause help copy was shortened to fit beside settings.

`tools/check-runtime-audio-resume.cjs` loads the production audio system, music transport, lifecycle, pause menu and loop with controlled browser/device fixtures. It checks sixteen owner scenarios: ordinary resume; never-settling resume and bounded Mac control recovery; late recovery while running; late completion after pause/stop/title; immediate cancellation by each of those actions; a pause arriving at the deadline receipt; Mac losing degraded permission while waiting; safe retry in a beat-dependent mode; a new session; rejected audio permission; and three actual gesture-listener recovery/pause cases. It also checks the one-frame owner, frame-time reset and audio deadline cleanup. These checks do not establish actual browser device recovery or speaker output.

The protected music contract records only the two intentionally revised pause/resume method hashes, with their prior hashes and this scope. The source-start/loop methods, timer scheduler, music transport bytes and complete profile remain pinned to their previous values.
